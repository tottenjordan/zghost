[merge_planners]: # Summary of Campaign and Trend Research

## Gen Z Consumer Behavior & Preferences

Gen Z is emerging as the primary catalyst for the "eco-conscious" transformation within the household goods sector. Approximately **60% of Gen Z** have ceased purchasing from brands that lack transparent environmental credentials, indicating that sustainability is a foundational expectation. This demographic prioritizes biodegradable ingredients, minimal and recyclable packaging, and a reduced carbon footprint. Furthermore, around **73% of Gen Z** are prepared to pay a premium for sustainable products, with many accepting a **10% price increase** for eco-friendly or organic alternatives. They also favor concentrated formulas for their efficiency, reducing storage needs and plastic waste. While Gen Z drives social media narratives, Baby Boomers and higher-income groups also demonstrate a strong willingness to pay for sustainable detergent innovations.

## Scent Trends & Emotional Connection

Fragrance has evolved beyond a mere functional aspect, becoming a means of emotional self-expression, particularly for younger demographics. The "Hibiscus" appeal, alongside other "tropical-sweet" scents like vanilla and cherry blossom, resonates with **64% of Gen Z**, who perceive these as modern and identity-driven. A significant **80% of Gen Z** select products based on the emotional response the scent evokes, such as confidence, nostalgia, or relaxation. Hibiscus, with its association with tropical escapism, aligns with these wellness-oriented goals. The rising "Smellmaxxing" trend sees Gen Z layering multiple scents to create a personalized aroma, demanding long-lasting and high-performance fragrances. This highlights that "freshness" is now tied to a long-lasting sensory experience. YouTube trends reinforce this, showing a strong consumer preference for plant-based scents like hibiscus over synthetic perfumes, which are seen as natural, functional alternatives.

## Sustainability & Performance Paradox

A significant challenge lies in the "sustainability-performance gap." Research indicates that consumers will revert to less eco-friendly behaviors (e.g., rewashing, using hotter water) if sustainable products fail to perform effectively (e.g., clothes not feeling soft or smelling fresh). Tide, being a recognized performance leader, needs to clearly communicate that its plant-based Hibiscus formula maintains the expected "2x cleaning power" and softness without compromise. While there's a desire for eco-friendly options, a notable "attitude-behavior gap" exists where many users return to Tide for its reliable performance on tough stains.

## Packaging & Transparency

Packaging is a crucial indicator of a brand's sustainability efforts. **90% of Gen Z** are willing to pay more for sustainable packaging, favoring materials like mycelium, hemp waste, and Post-Consumer Recycled (PCR) plastics as high-trust signals. This generation is highly skeptical of "greenwashing," with **91% believing brands engage in deceptive environmental claims**. They demand radical transparency, often seeking information through **QR codes** or "Digital Product Passports" to verify ingredient sourcing and recyclability. The shift to lightweight "laundry sheets" by disruptor brands further illustrates a move towards minimalist, plastic-free, and aesthetically pleasing packaging.

## Social Media & "CleanTok" Influence

Social media, particularly TikTok, plays a pivotal role in shaping laundry habits and product discovery. Over a third of Gen Z derive their laundry advice from TikTok, with in-app shopping and influencer-led "cleaning hacks" serving as primary channels. The **#CleanTok** movement, boasting over 150 billion views, has popularized routines like "laundry stripping" and "Sunday Resets," transforming chores into "eco-self-care." This trend also highlights the "eco-influencer" as a key authority, and content is trending due to the rising interest in "low-tox" living and zero-waste packaging. Gen Z values authentic, unpolished reviews over traditional high-budget advertisements, being **62% more likely** to disregard content perceived as artificial. Visual aesthetics and "shelf-appeal" are crucial, as products that align with a laundry room "aesthetic" are more likely to go viral. The "CleanTok" phenomenon has evolved to embody "lifestyle curation," where every product must align with sustainable values, driving a "premiumization" of the laundry category.

## Market Landscape & Tide's Position

The fabric softener market is projected to reach **$30.62 Billion by 2035**, with organic/plant-based segments experiencing the fastest growth. This growth is driven by increasing awareness of issues like microplastic pollution (synthetic textiles release up to **700,000 microfibers per wash**) and a preference for "low-tox" living, where **45% of consumers** prioritize non-toxic and biodegradable ingredients. Challenger brands like Earth Breeze, Tru Earth, Blueland, and HeySunday are disrupting the market with innovative, plastic-free solutions like laundry sheets. Major players, including Tide with its Evo detergent tiles, are responding to this "plastic-free" movement. Regulatory changes, such as France's mandate for microfiber filters and the U.S. Fighting Fibers Act, are also driving systemic shifts. While Tide has built trust through its "Ambition 2030" goals, winning over "green-natives" requires continued distancing from synthetic chemicals and oil-derived fragrances, which are increasingly seen as deal-breakers.

## Strategic Implications for Tide Hibiscus Scent

The Tide Hibiscus Scent campaign should capitalize on the "Eco-Self-Care" narrative, bridging Tide's renowned high-performance cleaning with the sensory, botanical experience desired by modern "CleanTok" audiences. The opportunity lies in targeting the "low-tox curious" consumer who seeks the aesthetic and premium scent of botanical products without sacrificing the reliability of a heritage brand.
